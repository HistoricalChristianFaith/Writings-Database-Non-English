# Origen, *Homilies on Joshua* (*In Iesu Nave*) — Latin source (Rufinus's version)

Twenty-six homilies of Origen on the book of Joshua, surviving not in the lost Greek but
in the Latin translation made by **Rufinus of Aquileia** (c. 400). This folder holds the
Latin base text used for the English translation in the main repository.

## Base text
- **Edition:** W. A. Baehrens (ed.), *Origenes Werke* VII — *Homilien zum Hexateuch in
  Rufins Übersetzung*, 2. Teil, **GCS 30** (Leipzig, 1921), *In Iesu Nave homiliae I–XXVI*.
- **Digital source:** the public-domain scan of *Origenes Werke* Bd. 7 (1921) on
  archive.org; the running text was reconstructed from its DjVu OCR.
- **Public domain.** The 1921 edition is long out of copyright; a fresh English
  translation from this text is clean to publish.

## Collation
Every homily was collated **line by line against the printed pages** of GCS 30 (the
critical edition's main text, not Migne/PG 12 and not the apparatus variants). Earlier
cleanup passes had left silent OCR residue in the running text — dropped words, wrong-but-
valid word swaps, base-vs-variant readings, and mis-scoped scripture marking — which this
collation caught and corrected against Baehrens's printed line.

## What was stripped
Removed from the running text: the critical apparatus, running heads, marginal notes, and
page numbers. Kept: all of Rufinus's running Latin.

## Editorial marks
- `« … »` wrap the scriptural quotations and echoes that the printed page marks with
  `» … «` (Baehrens's German-oriented guillemets, flipped to French orientation here).
- `[ … ]` marks text **editorially restored** where the OCR had dropped or garbled it,
  confirmed against the apparatus, the scripture reference, and Bruce's translation.
- Baehrens's spellings are kept (Iesus, Istrahel, Moyses, Iordanen, Hierusalem,
  coelum/coelestium, adscendere, coheres, exstrui …); printed *J* is normalized to *I*.

## Structure markers
- `== HOMILIA N ==` headings (N = 1–26). Where the edition prints a descriptive title
  (`De eo, quod scriptum est …`), it is kept on the line below the heading.
- `[§N]` markers follow **Baehrens's printed section numbering** within each homily.

## Files
- `homily01_latin.txt` … `homily26_latin.txt` — the twenty-six homilies, in order.
